## In one line
The real glueball spectrum of pure SU(3) Yang–Mills drawn as **a light cone whose tip is empty**: one point for the vacuum, a fan of curves for the particles, a ruled plane for everything above twice the lightest mass, and a red bar measuring the silence between the vacuum and the first particle, the mass gap Δ.

## Lede
The glueball spectrum of Yang–Mills theory as **a light cone with an empty tip**: one point for the vacuum, a red bar measuring the mass gap above it.

## On the sheet
A single black dot sits low at the left. A heavy red bar rises from it and turns into a red curve sweeping up to the right, above a grey dashed diagonal. Five fine black curves fan out higher, under a dense field of horizontal black rules across the upper half. Small tags label each curve; the title is at the top left.

## The science
Height is energy and width is momentum. The red curve is the lightest glueball, its start height is the gap, and the grey diagonal is where massless particles would travel; nothing sits on it. The black curves use lattice-computed masses; the ruled field is the two-particle continuum. That this is a proof problem, and omitted states, are stated in text.

## The science it encodes
The sheet is the (momentum, energy) plane of the quantum theory, height energy and width momentum, at one scale (100 mm per Δ on the design sheet), so light moves at 45°. Only p ≥ 0 is drawn; rotation symmetry makes that half the whole truth.

- **The point** is the vacuum: zero energy, zero momentum, the apex of the light cone.
- **The grey dashed diagonal** is that cone, E = |p|, where the classical Yang–Mills waves would travel. It is dashed and grey because nothing quantum sits on it: no massless gluon.
- **The red curve** is the lightest glueball, 0⁺⁺, E = √(p² + Δ²). Its vertex height is Δ, and the **red bar** measures it from the vacuum. The empty wedge between cone and red curve is the gap itself: no state between 0 and Δ.
- **The five black curves** are heavier glueballs (2⁺⁺, 0⁻⁺, 0⁺⁺*, 1⁺⁻, 2⁻⁺), each a mass shell placed at its continuum-limit lattice mass (1.437 to 1.856 Δ). The uneven gaps are measured, not designed.
- **The ruled plane** is the two-glueball continuum above E = √(p² + 4Δ²); the rim is its edge at 2Δ, not a particle.

What is only stated in text: that this is a *proof* problem (the physics is not in doubt), the data source, and the omissions (2⁺⁺* merged into the rim, states above 2Δ left out). Multiplicities and error bars are not encoded.

## How it got here
r01 (faithful) and r02 (abstract) were built in parallel from one encoding; r01 read as a centred goblet and ranked third. This render is r02, the half-section with one ghost diagonal and a heavy red Δ bar; it failed art (7.00, min 5) because the tags on the axis read as y-axis ticks and the small vacuum point read as the bar's foot. r03 iterated on it (a Ø9.5 mm point, tags moved to line ends, one momentum crop) and became the first science PASS, but its text volume grew, so r04 is planned as a subtraction round.
