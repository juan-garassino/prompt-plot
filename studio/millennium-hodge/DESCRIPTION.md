## In one line
Plate 5 of the Millennium series draws the one place the Hodge conjecture can actually be seen: a curved surface built **only from two families of straight blue and gold strings**, on which every curve, even a perfect circle, has the same class as a sum of those strings. A row of five small copies shows the classes [A], [B], [A]+[B], [A]+2[B] and 2[A]+3[B] as real curves.

## Lede
Plate five shows a surface built only from straight lines, where **every curve on it is a sum of two kinds of string**, even a circle.

## On the sheet
A weave of crossing blue and gold strings forms a hyperboloid at the upper right, with a bare lens of paper at its middle and green curves lying across it. Black text sits down the left. Along the lower part, five small copies show a line, a second line, a conic, a twisted cubic and a quintic, with a green circle, an equals sign and blue and gold slashes beneath.

## The science
The surface is one of the simplest curved surfaces, and it carries exactly two families of straight lines. Every curve drawn on it can be counted as so many lines of one family plus so many of the other, which is one of the cases where the Hodge conjecture has been a theorem since 1924. The drawing is exact; the general statement is text only.

## The science it encodes
The Hodge conjecture asks whether every rational Hodge class on a smooth complex projective variety is a rational combination of classes of subvarieties. The sheet does not draw an open case, since those begin in real dimension 8. It draws a case where the answer is already a theorem, and draws it exactly.

The surface is the quadric P¹×P¹. Its real points are the hyperboloid x² + y² − z² = 1, clipped to |z| ≤ 2. Every blue string is a straight line of one ruling, with class [A]. Every gold string is a line of the other ruling, with class [B]. The surface's curvature exists only as the envelope of these lines. Here h^{2,0} = 0, so every class is a Hodge class, and each one is a combination a[A] + b[B]. This is Lefschetz's (1,1) theorem of 1924.

The green curves are cut by planes turning about the tangent line at one point. They run from the waist circle through ellipses and a parabola to hyperbolas, and all of them have class [A]+[B]. At the end of the turn, the curve becomes one blue string plus one gold string, which is the heavy X. The rebus states the result as classes: [circle] = [A] + [B]. The drawing shows the family only up to its last hyperbola. The row shows five genuine curves with their exact classes. The Hodge decomposition, the complex points and the conjecture itself appear only as text.
