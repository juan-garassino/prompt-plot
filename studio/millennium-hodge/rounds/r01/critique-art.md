## Reads at a glance

At 3 m a stranger sees a luminous blue-and-gold string model, like a hyperboloid, with a lens of bare paper through it, green curves draped across it, and a museum-style row of small copies underneath. It reads as a beautiful maths-model poster. It does not read as "the loops close onto an X".

## Acceptance checks (encoding §11)

1. **ONLY STRAIGHT STRINGS, NO OUTLINE: PASS (png).** There is no curved blue or gold anywhere, and no rim, contour or shading. The silhouette exists only as string ends. There is no solid band of one colour. The gcode collinearity check is left to the science critic.
2. **ONE PINCH POINT: FAIL.** The blue×gold X crosses at about (172, 286) mm. The lower-left green fan (five members) converges on the eye's LEFT TIP at about (154, 270) mm, 24 mm away. The right-hand bundle of four parallel arcs stops 6–17 mm short of p, on the far side of the eye's corner. On paper that makes two convergence sites, split by the throat corner, rather than one pinch.
3. **CIRCLE TO X, IN ORDER: FAIL.** The waist circle is drawn as arcs broken at both eye corners, so it is not a closed circle on paper. The big ellipse does graze the bottom rim's string ends (good). The hyperbolas' second branches appear as detached green U's in the upper lobe, at about (158–190, 344–359) mm and (140–229, 300–352) mm. The outward order circle → ellipses → open → X cannot be read, because the members split around p, and the terminal X is visually lopsided (blue arm about 69 mm, gold about 188 mm).
4. **THE EYE: PASS.** It is a bare-paper lens of 78 × 33 mm (minor axis 33 ≥ 28), about 1690 mm² in area, and is bounded only by string ends and green. (F) The row cells repeat the same eye shape and tilt at scale. Blemish: blue fragments intrude at the left corner.
5. **HONEST ROW AND HONEST WORDS: PASS**, with a note. The captions read exactly [A] · [B] · [A]+[B] · [A]+2[B] · 2[A]+3[B]. The cubic exits the right rim and the quintic exits the top rim. Both are broken mid-surface by visibility cuts: the quintic is 4 fragments and reads scribbly, and the cubic's lower hook ends inside the weave. The text contains RATIONAL, PROJECTIVE, THEOREM (LEFSCHETZ 1924) and REAL DIMENSION 8. No colour is attached to H^{p,q}. The rebus reads green ○ = blue ╲ + gold ╱, with slants of 31° and 35° against the X's 32° and 36°, so they are parallel.

The interpretation is honest: the ribbon becomes the variety, the hole becomes the eye, and C₀…C₄ become genuine classes. The architecture was inherited wholesale from the AI poster, however: column, hero, deck and footer. This is the "scientific figure" failure mode wearing Gabo's strings. Hung beside *Linear Construction No. 1*, the hero holds its own and the sheet around it does not.

HANDOFF deviations noted, not scored: roll −30° (encoding −12°) and cell N = 32 (encoding 16). Roll −30° may be what makes the blue X counter-thrust nearly horizontal and short on the sheet.

## Biggest weakness

The pencil does not close onto the X on paper. The green members converge on the eye's left tip, 24 mm from the blue×gold crossing. The circle is broken, and the blue X-arm is a stub. So the plate's single sentence, "a circle slid through its family becomes two straight strings", is not visible. The viewer sees green curves draped over a string model, not a family collapsing to a point and an X.

## Mandates

1. **One pinch, at the crossing.** Every one of the six green members, plus both X strings, must visibly converge on the blue×gold crossing (currently about (172, 286) mm). Each green stroke's end nearest p must lie 5–16 mm from that crossing, staggered, and ON BOTH SIDES of p. The waist circle must reach the crossing. No green stroke may end at the eye's left tip (about (154, 270) mm). Test: extend each green end along its tangent, and they must meet within a 3 mm disc on the X crossing. Fix it through the view (α₀ / elevation), not by redrawing hidden stretches.
2. **A two-armed X.** The blue index-0 string's visible length must be at least 0.8× the gold's (now about 69 mm against 188 mm), and it must cross the gold at 70–110° on the sheet, so the X reads at 3 m as two comparable heavy lines and is the heaviest mark in the hero, heavier than any green. Re-measure after the view change in mandate 1. If roll −30° is what flattened the blue arm, return toward the encoding's −12°.
3. **Kill the dead band.** Move the hero down so its lowest string end sits at y = 136–140 mm, leaving one gutter of about 12 mm (the row's cell gutter, 11.75 mm) above the row header at y = 124. Then re-seat the left column on that grid: the key's top line aligns with the hero's leftmost string end, and `H^k(X,C)` goes to the empty band y ≈ 150–175 or is cut. The vacated strip at the top right, between the corner caption (y ≈ 398) and the hero's top, becomes the deliberate quiet zone opposite the title. Test: no empty full-width horizontal band taller than 15 mm anywhere between y = 124 and y = 378.
