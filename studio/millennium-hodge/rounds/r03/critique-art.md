## Reads at a glance
At 3 m you see a blue-and-gold string hyperboloid with a lens of bare paper through its throat, a fan of green curves pinched onto one heavy gold/blue X, and a big bracketed `[○] = [╱] + [╲]` in the corner. At 30 cm the green loops do not read as circle → ellipse → X.

## Acceptance checks
Encoding §11 (thesis A):
1. ONLY STRAIGHT STRINGS, NO OUTLINE: **PASS**. No blue or gold curve and no rim or contour line. The silhouette is read from string ends only, and no unintended solid band appears.
2. ONE PINCH POINT: **PASS**. Green and both X strings meet at p (≈169.7, 166.1). There are staggered stops on both arms (about 8, 12 and 24 mm on the left) and no green blot. One green curve and the X pass through p.
3. CIRCLE TO X, IN ORDER: **FAIL**. Every closed member is broken at the eye corners (right corner gap ≈ 6–7 mm). The ψ=31.72° graze at (195.9, 227.6) is in-frame but reads as an orphan crescent, because the bottom-rim string ends around it are LOD-thinned away. The lower-lip arc (150–199, 182–196) ends mid-lip at both ends. At 30 cm the order cannot be read.
4. THE EYE: **FAIL**. Its height, about 52 mm, is ≥ 45 mm. But it does not contain zero ink: the green crescent at x 167–205, y 225–234 lies 1–6 mm below the nearest string ends, on bare paper. The gold X is the longest straight line (≈ 220 mm against ≈ 205 mm for the blue) and is unbroken. Note that it runs rim to rim, not crop to rim, because there is no lower-left crop.
5. HONEST WORDS: **PASS** (with flag). RATIONAL, PROJECTIVE, THEOREM (LEFSCHETZ 1924) and REAL DIMENSION 8 are all present, and no colour is attached to H^{p,q}. The rebus reads green ○ = blue ╱ + gold ╲, with slants parallel to the X (+18° / −49°). Flag: the key's "ALL ARE BUILT FROM [A] AND [B]" leans toward the §8/§9.9 misconception ("represented", not "built from"). This is for the science critic and the lead, because the wording came from S2(c).

AUTHORING §6 (reference in play; the plate is judged as an interpretation):
1. Forms recognisable without fills? **Yes**. The quadric is legible from rulings alone.
2. Do shadow lines follow the surface? **Yes**. There is no shading, and every line is a ruling.
3. Fine lines that are two sides of one thick stroke? **Yes, the X**. The blue 7-pass band shows as 4–5 separate hairlines.
4. Are the blackest regions intended? **Yes**. The X bands and throat corners are the densest zones, and the corners are relieved by LOD.
5. Labels readable at pen width? **Yes**. Black 0.3 type is crisp, and the tags are off the weave.
6. Does a thicker pen knot or fill the eye highlight? **Yes, once**. The 0.5 green crescent sits inside the eye.
7. Long travels or pointless tiny marks? Minor. There are a few 8–13 mm hairline stubs along the upper lip (x 145–215, y 228–236) and 10 m of travel. Acceptable.

## Biggest weakness
The pencil does not read as a sequence from circle to X. The closed members are broken at the throat corners, the one piece of evidence of the graze (the 31.72° crescent) floats in the eye's bare paper instead of touching string ends, and the plate's punchline therefore reads as "a fan of green curves near an X". The eye, the plate's quiet zone, now carries ink as well.

## Mandates
1. **Make the graze a kiss and empty the eye.** The green crescent at x 167–205, y 225–234 must touch string ends, not paper. Keep at least 6 bottom-rim string ends (either family) ending within 1 mm of it along x 175–205: exempt the far-rim strings there from LOD thinning. If that cannot be done, move the view so the graze vertex sits on a string-carrying envelope. Test: zoom (160–215, 218–240). No green run has bare paper on both sides for more than 5 mm, and the lens below the string ends holds zero ink.
2. **Fuse the X into two equal heavy bands.** Tighten the pass offsets until each X band is one solid stroke of about 0.6–0.7 mm. Test: at 12 px/mm, show no paper gap inside the blue band at (150–200, 160–185) or the gold band at (240–255, 80–100). At arm's length the blue arm must read darker than any 0.5 green curve (today it reads lavender). While changing the view or roll, bring the crossing to ≥ 70° (today it is 67.6°).
3. **Restore the swallow: kill the dead band and the symmetric header.** Scale the construction up (k) so its top rim rises to within 15 mm of the rebus's lower edge (y ≈ 345), and let it crop at a second frame edge (left-lower or bottom, as in r02 and §5A) without entering the text column. Test: the construction touches ≥ 2 frame lines, and no empty rectangle ≥ 150 × 40 mm exists outside the eye and the text column. Alternatively, drop the rebus onto the top rim's shoulder so the title and rebus no longer share one band.

## Regressions vs compare-to
- **Scale and swallow lost.** r02's construction filled the sheet (top rim at y ≈ 346, cropped at right and bottom). r03 is a contained object: the top rim is at y ≈ 302, the bottom rim sits entirely on the sheet at y ≈ 24, and only the right frame crops it. This opens the dead band at y 305–360. It is the main reason tension drops from r02's 8 to 7.
- **Ink in the eye.** r02's eye held zero ink (science-verified). r03 puts the 0.5 green crescent inside it.
- **Header became symmetric.** r02's open, unbracketed rebus trailed off the title band with some lean. r03's bracketed rebus is a matched block opposite the title, the same height and the same band. The brackets are correct (S2), but the placement is static.
