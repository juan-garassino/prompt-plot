## In one line
Plate 7 of the Millennium series puts an elliptic curve and its L-function on one sheet at one scale: **a single gold point generates every rational point by drawing lines, and the L-function crosses zero once, in gold, exactly where the conjecture says it must.**

## The science it encodes
The curve is Cremona 37a1, y² + y = x³ − x, drawn exactly. It has two real components, the egg and the open branch, and both are mirror images about y = −½. Its rational points form a group generated by one point, P = (0,0) (rank 1). The drawing shows how the group works: a line through P and nP meets the curve once more, and reflecting that third point in y = −½ gives (n+1)P. Every open circle is a true multiple nP for |n| ≤ 30. Odd multiples land on the egg and even ones on the branch, and that parity is visible as position.

The L-function is computed, not sketched. L(0) = 0, L is negative on (0, 1), and it crosses zero transversally at s = 1. Its axis lies on the curve's own mirror height, and one millimetre scale serves x, y, s and L, so the gold crossing leans at 17.0°, which is arctan L′(E,1). That slope is shown as geometry. Three things are stated only in text: the conjecture itself (rank = order of vanishing), the leading-term identity L′(E,1) = real period × height of P = 5.98692 × 0.05111, and the fact that the orbit never ends. The sheet draws 47 points of infinitely many.
