## 0. Translator's measurement (this changes the dossier's picture, so read it first)

I measured the embedded isochrones on `neckpinch.npz` using `ricci_rot.embed`, closed
outlines (z, ±ρ) and a point-in-polygon test. The results:

- **Before surgery the isochrones do NOT nest.** The space gets *longer* while its neck
  pinches: pole-to-pole goes from 4.001 to 4.335 units (t = 0 → t_s). Under every rigid
  alignment tried (neck, ψ²-centroid, either pole, mid-poles), each later outline sticks out
  past the earlier one near a pole, by 0.018–0.048 u per Δt = 0.005. A dossier-style "one nest
  crowding at the neck" would be a braid of crossings.
- **After surgery the pieces DO nest** once each is pinned at its own ψ²ds-centroid. B is
  perfect (margin > 0). A sticks out at most 0.011 u (≈ 0.65 mm) for the first few steps,
  which is under the floor, so the merge rule absorbs it.
- **The caps retract fast.** A's cap tip goes +6.4 → −0.8 → −10.6 → −17.0 mm (at 55 mm/u)
  relative to the cut, at t = t_s, 0.060, 0.065, 0.070. B is the mirror image. A time grid
  on multiples of 0.01 would put the t = 0.06 rings *inside* the cut circle, so **the grid
  is anchored at t_s** (see §4).

The dossier's truth stands. Its geometry is resolved into **one keyline** (the surgery
instant): **the past lies outside it as a halo, and the future lies inside it as two nests.**

## 3. The abstract ORDER

**BRANCHING NEST → two FLOW-TO-ATTRACTOR nests, stacked by time-occlusion.**

The mapping in one line: **each closed line is the whole space at one instant, drawn in
meridian section. One line = Δt 0.01 of Ricci time counted from the cut. Past lines lie
outside the keyline and future lines inside it. The red circle is the cut, and each red point
is where one piece vanishes.**

**Time-occlusion rule (binding):** each isochrone is an opaque card, and later cards lie on
top of earlier ones. A line is drawn only where it is not under a later card. This one rule:

- clips the pre-surgery halo where the lengthening space overruns it near the poles. That is
  about 21–24 % of each past line, and up to ≈ 17 mm deep at A's pole under neck-pinning;
- keeps the future nests whole;
- gives the plate its depth read: two terraced mounds whose height is remaining lifetime.

The past lines "land" on the keyline instead of crossing it. NOTES must state the clip and
quote the pole-to-pole growth 4.001 → 4.335.

It is not a plot, because nothing is extruded. It is not an object, because no named thing is
depicted.

**Merge rule (one rule, structural):** lines are ranked by |t − t_s|, closest first, and the
two event lines are ranked above everything. Each lower-ranked line **pauses** wherever it
comes within 0.8 mm of a higher-ranked drawn line, and resumes when clear. Use `Occupancy` +
`lines(mode="pause_resume")`. Fragments shorter than 2 mm are culled. So rings peel away from
the keyline where the geometry is still, and separate where it races.

**Data (binding):** re-run the solver from a copy of `data/run_neckpinch.py` in the round
directory, with snapshots at **exactly t = 0, t_s ± 0.01k** (deterministic: t_s reproduces).
Report max |t_snap − t_target|. If it exceeds 1e-4, interpolate between the two bracketing
*solver steps*, never between the 0.005 npz snapshots. The first three post-surgery rings may
not be interpolated at all, because the caps move 7–10 mm per 0.005.

Deliberately not encoded: curvature R as tone, the 2-D control as a drawing, the planar CSF

## 10. Fabrication

- **Floor:** 0.8 mm between any two drawn lines of one pen, measured on the gcode (0.3 nib:
  2.4 × 0.3 = 0.72, so 0.8 governs). It is held by the merge rule, not by respacing. At 60 mm/u:
  halo steps are 0.64 (merges) / 1.5–3.4 mm, A ring steps ≥ 1.45 mm at the equator (pausing at
  the far pole), and B ≥ 2.6 mm. `[faithful]` Re-check in screen space after projection:
  foreshortening along the axis tightens the far-pole crowding, and ScreenThin must cover the
  shell.
- **Stroke order (L1):** start line → halo from outer to inner → keyline ×2 → B rings from outer
  to inner → A rings from outer to inner. That is time order within each region, and every
  consecutive stroke is adjacent (no sheet-crossing travel). The longest stroke is the keyline,
  ≈ 710 mm ≈ 71 s, and it is a clean batch boundary. Re-zero points fall between the regions.
- **Budget** (Leo F600 = 10 mm/s, ≈ 2.5 s per lift/drop):

| layer | draw length | strokes | ≈ min |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[faithful]` L0 grey | ≈ 2 m | ≈ 80 | ≈ 7 |
| L1 black | halo ≈ 2.8 m + keyline 1.4 m + A ≈ 8.5 m + B ≈ 0.6 m ≈ **13.3 m** | ≈ 140 (with pause fragments) | 22 + 6 ≈ **28** |

- **Flood risks:**
  1. the waist, where the halo, keyline, caps and `[faithful]` loop meet within about 20 mm;
  2. A's far pole, where the rings pile up (the merge rule must run on the final mm geometry);

  4. the keyline double pass, which must retrace the identical path.
- **Determinism:** no randomness anywhere. `.gcode` goes beside every render. NOTES records the
  scale, alignment, β, snapshot tolerance, and the clipped fraction of each past line.
